\section{Overview}
The Pololu 3pi+ 32U4 robot has two DC motors, each driven by a DRV8838 driver with a direction pin (PH) and an enable pin (EN). In this lab, the motors were driven at full speed by switching EN on and off, then at quarter speed with Timer1 phase-correct PWM. All five Timer1 prescalers were tested to find the best PWM frequency.

\section{Procedure}
\begin{itemize}

\item \textbf{Setup} Table~\ref{tab:pins} lists the Port B pins. The motor pins start low, which brakes both motors. Button A uses the internal pull-up, and the program waits for a press of A before moving.

\begin{table}[H]
  \centering
  \caption{Port B pins used in this lab.}
  \label{tab:pins}
  \begin{tabular}{lll}
    \toprule
    Pin & Function & Mode \\
    \midrule
    PB1 & Right motor direction (PH) & Output \\
    PB2 & Left motor direction (PH) & Output \\
    PB3 & Button A & Input, pull-up \\
    PB5 & Right motor enable (EN) & Output, Timer1 OC1A \\
    PB6 & Left motor enable (EN) & Output, Timer1 OC1B \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\item \textbf{Full-speed tests} With TCCR1A cleared, the motor pins are written directly (Table~\ref{tab:patterns}). By writing EN high and then PH = 0 for forward or PH = 1 for reverse, the robot moves in alternating directions for 1~s each time. Between each move, EN is pulled low, which brakes the robot for 3~s.

\begin{table}[H]
  \centering
  \caption{Port B motor bits for each move (bits 6, 5, 2, 1 are left EN, right EN, left PH, right PH).}
  \label{tab:patterns}
  \begin{tabular}{lc}
    \toprule
    Move & Binary \\
    \midrule
    Brake & 00000000 \\
    Left forward & 01000000 \\
    Left reverse & 01000100 \\
    Right forward & 00100000 \\
    Right reverse & 00100010 \\
    Both forward & 01100000 \\
    Both reverse & 01100110 \\
    Spin CCW (left reverse, right forward) & 01100100 \\
    Spin CW (left forward, right reverse) & 01100010 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\item \textbf{Quarter-speed tests} Timer1 runs in 8-bit phase-correct PWM (Mode~1, TOP = 255), non-inverting on OC1A and OC1B (TCCR1A = 0xA1). CS12:10 select the prescaler $N$, and since the counter counts up to TOP and back down,
\begin{equation}
  f_\text{PWM} = \frac{16~\text{MHz}}{2 \cdot N \cdot \text{TOP}} = \frac{16~\text{MHz}}{2 \cdot N \cdot 255}.
  \label{eq:fpwm}
\end{equation}
Quarter speed is $\text{OCR1x} = 64$ (25\% duty). Each move runs for 2~s, then brakes for 3~s.

\item \textbf{Prescaler experiment} After each quarter-speed run, pressing A switches to the next prescaler and repeats the test.
\end{itemize}

\section{Post-Lab}
\question{Comment on any unexpected behaviors exhibited by the robot during the experiments.}
$N = 8$ felt smoother than $N = 1$, which was expected to be the smoothest (explained below). Otherwise, the robot behaved as expected. The only difference was that $N = 8$ produced a slight whine in the motor.

\question{Explain the difference in behavior between non-inverting and inverting control methods.}
Non-inverting output is high while $\text{TCNT1} < \text{OCR1x}$ (duty $= \text{OCR1x}/255$), and inverting output is high while $\text{TCNT1} > \text{OCR1x}$ (duty $= 1 - \text{OCR1x}/255$). Since EN low brakes, the same OCR value gives opposite speeds:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Non-inverting:} OCR1x = 0 brakes, 64 is quarter speed, and 255 is full speed.
\item \textbf{Inverting:} OCR1x = 255 brakes, 191 is quarter speed, and 0 is full speed.
\end{itemize}
For the same duty cycle, the motor behaves identically in both modes. Non-inverting makes more sense intuitively, since a larger OCR means a faster motor and 0 means stopped.

\question{Describe the results of part 3 from the in-lab portion for each of the prescaler values.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{$N = 1$ (31.4~kHz).} Above the 20~kHz limit of hearing, so silent. Motor feels and sounds smooth.
\item \textbf{$N = 8$ (3.92~kHz).} This is a normal frequency that humans can hear, which caused a whine.
\item \textbf{$N = 64$ (490~Hz) and $N = 256$ (123~Hz).} The current rises and falls each cycle, giving a torque pulse heard as a slight rattle.
\item \textbf{$N = 1024$ (30.6~Hz).} The 32.6~ms period lets the motor speed up and slow down each cycle, so the robot moved roughly.
\end{itemize}

\question{Justify your choice of the best value to use.}
$N = 1$, the only setting that is both silent and free of torque ripple. $N = 8$'s whine is a real drawback that puts it below $N = 1$. All other values produced weird torque rippling and sounds.

\section{Discussion/Conclusion}
The 3pi+ motors were driven at full speed with on/off outputs and at quarter speed with Timer1 phase-correct PWM, and every move went in the expected direction. $N = 1$ (31.4~kHz) was the best prescaler: silent and smooth, while $N = 8$ whined, $N = 64$ and $256$ rattled slightly, and $N = 1024$ was rough.
